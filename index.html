Analytical projects explained simply

A simple explanation of the problem, my contribution, and the purpose of each project.

End-to-end supply chain dashboard SQL · Tableau

What it does: Brings purchasing, inventory, and supplier information together so a planner can see where supply may fall short of production needs.

What I did: Built a SQL and Tableau dashboard combining open purchase orders, supplier lead times, on-hand inventory, on-time and in-full delivery performance, and shortage risk in one view.

Why it matters: It connects information that planning and procurement teams need to review together, helping them understand supply issues and decide where to focus their attention.

Inventory optimization model Python · Pandas · NumPy

What it does: Explores how much stock to order and when to reorder across a catalog of products, while balancing availability and inventory cost.

What I did: Built a Python model using Pandas and NumPy to calculate economic order quantities, safety stock, and reorder points. I simulated policy changes to examine their effect on total supply chain cost.

Why it matters: It makes the trade-off between service levels, holding costs, and obsolete-stock risk easier to evaluate before changing an inventory policy.

Demand forecasting & MRP simulation Python

What it does: Connects a demand forecast to a materials plan, showing how changes in demand or supplier timing can affect planned orders and stock coverage. MRP means material requirements planning.

What I did: Built moving-average and exponential-smoothing forecasts in Python and linked them to an MRP simulation. I evaluated planned orders, coverage, and sensitivity to lead times and supplier reliability.

Why it matters: It helps explain how forecasting assumptions flow into purchasing and replenishment decisions, making planning trade-offs easier to discuss.

Supplier segmentation & risk scoring Segmentation · Risk analysis

What it does: Groups suppliers by their business importance and supply risk, helping identify which relationships need closer attention.

What I did: Built a supplier-segmentation and risk-scoring model using spend, part criticality, lead time, on-time and in-full delivery performance, and reliance on a single source. I used a Kraljic-style approach to organize the supplier portfolio.

Why it matters: The model provides a structured basis for prioritizing strategic sourcing and risk-mitigation actions, rather than treating every supplier as equally critical.

Bullwhip effect simulation Python

What it does: Shows how a change in customer demand can create larger order swings as information moves from a retailer to a distributor and then to a manufacturer. This is the bullwhip effect.

What I did: Built a Python simulation covering those three supply chain stages. I tested information-sharing policies to explore ways to reduce demand variation and inventory swings.

Why it matters: It helps explain why teams need a shared view of demand when making ordering and inventory decisions.

S&OP scenario planner College project · Excel · Power BI

What it does: Puts expected demand, production capacity, and inventory targets into one planning view. S&OP means sales and operations planning: bringing teams together around an achievable plan.

What I did: For a college project, I built an Excel and Power BI scenario planner to compare demand, capacity, and inventory targets.

Why it matters: The academic project demonstrates how a common planning view could support discussion of trade-offs when demand, capacity, and inventory targets do not align.

Process improvement & line balancing Time studies · Material-flow analysis

What it does: Examines how tasks and materials move through a production process to find uneven workloads, waiting time, and interruptions to replenishment.

What I did: Analyzed cycle times, resource utilization, and material flow. I used the findings to recommend changes to the layout and allocation of work across the process.

Why it matters: The study addressed idle time and replenishment consistency, connecting detailed observations of the process to practical improvements in how work is organized and materials are supplied.
